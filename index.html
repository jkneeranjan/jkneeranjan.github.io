Vertical AI

Horizontal AI answers. Vertical AI does the job.

A general assistant can hold a conversation. A restaurant needs one that knows table 4 seats five, the kitchen closes at 22:00 and a party of ten needs a call back from the owner.

That knowledge (the trade's data model, its rules, its daily workflow) is the product. The AI is the interface to it. So I build one industry at a time, and the whole stack for it.

  1. 01

    Own the record, not the minutes

    Every answered call writes a real reservation into the business's own book. After a few weeks that book is how the business runs, and that is what keeps a customer, not the voice.

  2. 02

    Encode the trade's rules in code

    Table capacity and floor plans, one calendar per stylist, German fiscal law at the till, GDPR and consent before any call is recorded. Rules live in code and tests, not only in a prompt.

  3. 03

    Ship the whole stack

    Voice agent, telephony, booking engine, web dashboard, iPhone and Android apps, CI and compliance. One person, one codebase, live in production.

Lucidence AI

An AI receptionist and booking book for restaurants and salons. When nobody picks up, the call goes to the AI. It books against the business's real tables, opening hours and stylists, in the caller's language, and puts everything into the owner's app.

  • Speaks 85 languages; books, moves and cancels appointments through tool calls into the business's own calendar
  • Restaurant capacity model (tables, floor plan, party size); one calendar per stylist for salons
  • SMS confirmation, reminder and review request; call summaries and callback requests for the owner
  • Platform rules a business can't override, screening of the owner's instructions, card numbers and IBANs redacted, every call checked afterwards
  • Multi-tenant PostgreSQL with automated cross-tenant isolation tests; about 490 server tests plus browser tests on every pull request
